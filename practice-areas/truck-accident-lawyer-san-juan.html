Why a truck case is different

A loaded 18-wheeler can weigh 20 or 30 times more than a car. That is why, when it crashes, the injuries are far more serious. But the legal difference is even bigger: behind the driver there is a company, its insurers, and its lawyers, who activate immediately to limit what they pay.

Facing that alone, while you recover, is nearly impossible. These cases are won with speed and with knowledge of how trucking companies operate.

The evidence that disappears fast

In a truck accident, key evidence can disappear in days if no one formally requests it:

  • The truck's "black box," which records speed and braking.
  • The driver's hours-of-service logs.
  • The vehicle's maintenance records.
  • The truck's cameras or those of nearby businesses.
  • The cargo records and its weight.

Who can be held responsible

Unlike a car crash, there can be several responsible parties, and that can mean more than one policy to cover your damages:

  • The truck driver.
  • The trucking company that employs them.
  • The owner of the truck, if different.
  • The company that loaded the cargo.
  • The manufacturer, if a part failed.

The federal rules that protect your case

Commercial trucks are governed by federal rules (the FMCSA): how many hours a driver can drive, how the vehicle must be maintained, how cargo must be secured. When a company breaks those rules and causes an accident, that greatly strengthens your case.

Knowing which rules apply and how to prove they were violated is part of what makes the difference between a low offer and fair compensation.
