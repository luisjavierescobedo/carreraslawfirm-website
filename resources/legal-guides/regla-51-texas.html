The 51% rule in Texas, explained simply

If you were hurt in an accident and wonder whether you can still claim when you were partly at fault, this rule is the one that decides. Here we explain it clearly, with a Valley example.

Direct answer

In Texas you can recover compensation for an accident as long as your responsibility is 50% or less. If you are found 51% or more at fault, the law bars you from recovering anything. That is why it is called the 51% rule.

The percentage of fault is not decided on its own: it is argued with evidence. Preparing a case well (the reports, witnesses, expert analysis) is what keeps your responsibility low and your right to claim intact.

What is the 51% rule in Texas?

It is the rule that decides whether you can claim when you shared some blame.

Texas uses a system called modified comparative negligence. In plain terms: the law recognizes that in many accidents more than one person bore some responsibility. As long as your share of the blame is 50% or less, you keep the right to receive compensation. The moment you cross to 51% or more, that right is lost entirely.

An example with numbers, on Expressway 83

Picture a crash on Expressway 83, in McAllen. Your total damages are found to be $100,000. Here is how everything changes with your percentage of fault:

  • At 0% fault: you recover the full $100,000.
  • At 30% fault: your compensation is reduced by that 30%, and you receive $70,000.
  • At 51% fault: you cross the line and receive $0. Nothing.
Here is the key point
The difference between receiving $70,000 and receiving nothing can come down to how your percentage of fault is argued. It is not a fixed number: it is something fought with evidence.

How is my percentage of fault decided?

The insurer does not decide it on its own, though it would love to. In a case, the percentage is established from the evidence:

  • The police accident report.
  • Witness statements.
  • Traffic or nearby business cameras.
  • Accident reconstruction expert analysis.

That is why the other driver's insurer works so hard to raise your percentage: every point they pin on you saves them money. And that is why having someone gathering evidence on your side changes the outcome.

Why does this matter so much in your case?

Because the 51% rule is, in practice, the insurers' main weapon. If they can push you above 50%, they owe you nothing, and they know how to try from the very first call. The way to protect yourself is not to face that game alone.
