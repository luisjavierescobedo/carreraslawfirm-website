How much is my car accident case worth in Texas?

There is no fixed number. Value depends on your injuries, who was at fault, and the insurance available. Here is what you can recover, explained simply.

Short answer

Texas has no chart and no single formula. Your case value is built by adding your economic damages (medical bills, lost wages, vehicle damage) and your non-economic damages (pain, suffering, the impact on your life). That total can be reduced by your share of fault and is almost always capped by the available insurance. A lawyer can give you a realistic range once they know your injuries and the coverage.

What you can recover

Texas law recognizes two broad types of damages.

Economic damages

  • Medical bills, past and future: ambulance, hospital, imaging, therapy and surgery.
  • Lost wages and the loss of your ability to earn if you cannot return to the same work.
  • Property damage, such as the repair or total loss of your vehicle.
  • Out-of-pocket costs tied to the crash.

Non-economic damages

  • Physical pain and suffering.
  • Mental anguish and emotional harm.
  • Physical impairment and disfigurement if you were left with lasting effects.
  • Loss of enjoyment of your daily life.

In cases with especially serious conduct, such as a drunk driver, the law sometimes allows exemplary damages. These are the exception and are subject to caps.

What makes a case worth more or less

  • How serious your injuries are and whether they leave permanent effects.
  • Your medical treatment and its total cost, now and in the future.
  • How clear the other driver's fault is.
  • The time you lost from work and its effect on your income.
  • The insurance available, both the at-fault driver's and your own.

Texas uses a comparative negligence rule: if you were partly at fault, your compensation is reduced by that percentage, and if you were more than 50% responsible you cannot recover anything. That is why nailing down fault changes the outcome so much.

The insurance ceiling

Even if your case is worth a lot, what you can actually collect usually depends on the available policies: the at-fault driver's, your own uninsured or underinsured motorist coverage, and other sources. Finding every available dollar of coverage is part of your lawyer's job.

Watch out for the first offer
The insurance company almost always offers little at first, before the full cost of your injuries is known. Accepting quickly can close your case for far less than it is worth.
